# Review Row Example

This generic example demonstrates the relationship between fields. It is not a mandatory customer-support procedure. Derive actual working methods from the user's current sources.

**Situation:** A customer asks a question for the first time.

**Proposed way of working:** Read the question and surrounding conversation to understand the customer's goal and reported behavior. Reuse known product-version and environment information. Consult related conversations, requests, or incident records when deciding whether this continues an earlier issue or when prior handling matters. Ask only for information that is necessary now and still unknown.

**Decision to review:** Whether the information to inspect or request is appropriate: does it omit important context or require unnecessary research?

**What to check:**

- Current question and conversation: the customer's goal, reported behavior, and information already supplied.
- Existing customer records: whether the product version or environment changes the applicability of an answer.
- Related conversations or tickets, when relevant: previous questions and answers, handling conclusions, and outstanding commitments.
- Missing information: which specific unknown would affect the next step and therefore needs clarification.

**Example change request, for illustration only:** "Reuse recorded version and environment details. Check related tickets when the question concerns an earlier error or request; do not search the entire history for routine guidance."

**My comments or changes:** Leave this field empty in the delivered form.

## Why the fields match

The decision concerns how much information to inspect or request. The checks identify the relevant sources and what to verify, while the example changes that information scope.

If the decision instead concerns whether a colleague has accepted a handoff, check the person's explicit confirmation, accepted scope, unaccepted work, and remaining follow-up responsibility. Customer version and environment details would not establish acceptance.
